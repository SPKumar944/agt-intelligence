AGT Intelligence Platform Architecture

How AGT turns open, licensed and administrative data into retail site decisions a committee can defend. This paper describes the system, each data layer, the method and the models, and how the same foundation serves reports, city snapshots and the Data API.

1 · Overview

A radius ring is not a trade area

Most site decisions in India still start with a 3 or 5 km circle and a score nobody can explain. AGT replaces the circle with behaviour, the score with its drivers, and weeks of work with minutes.

The platform answers the four questions behind every new store: where customers come from, what a site will earn, what it will take from existing stores, and where the market is still open. Three design choices make the answers defensible.

2 · System architecture

One foundation, from external sources to every user

Data moves left to right. Sources are fetched once into a versioned store, joined and modelled in one engine, and served through three doors. Governance applies at every layer.

3Data layersSix layers, one hexagonal gridWhat each layer answers, how it is built, and what it adds to every hexagon

Every source becomes one of six data layers. Each layer is processed on its own path, then summarised onto the same H3 hexagons, so every model reads every layer in the same cells.

3.1Places and brandsWho sells what, and where

Who sells what, where. Two sources are merged, cleaned and classified for each client's format before they are counted per hexagon.

Answers

Who already sells near a site, which chain brands are present, and which businesses compete with or help the client's format.

How it is built

Overture and OpenStreetMap places are fetched by tile. The same place found in both is matched by name and distance and kept once. Places are sorted into categories, chain outlets are recognised by name rules, and rival, complement and anchor roles come from the business profile an analyst approves.

Each hexagon gets

Places by category, chain outlets by brand, rivals and complements, and rates per 10,000 residents.

Watch for

Mapping is thinner in small towns, so cities are compared on shares and rates rather than raw counts.

3.2PopulationWho lives within reach

Who lives nearby. In Bengaluru the layer is built bottom-up from administrative small-area counts; elsewhere it falls back to gridded population, labelled as modelled.

Answers

How many people live within reach of a site, and how densely.

How it is built

In Bengaluru adults are counted per official small area, scaled up to official totals because the source covers only part of the population, and then floating working adults and children are added. Where fewer than 15 small areas fall inside a catchment, gridded population from WorldPop and Meta HRSL is blended in. Other cities use the grids, labelled as modelled.

Each hexagon gets

Residents, density per km², which source was used, a modelled flag and the data version.

Watch for

Standard grids undercount dense older neighbourhoods; Figure 5 shows the size of the gap in south Bengaluru.

3.3Roads and travel timeHow far customers really are

How far customers really are. Catchments grow along the street network and stop at barriers a circle would ignore.

Answers

How long customers really take to reach a site, and where the catchment stops.

How it is built

OpenStreetMap road segments are joined into a network typed by road class. Railways, rivers and expressways are marked as barriers that can be crossed only at real crossings. Walk and drive minutes are computed from every cell to the site and cut into primary and secondary zones.

Each hexagon gets

Minutes to each site, walk and drive reach, zone membership and whether a barrier crossing is needed.

Watch for

Travel times are modelled from the road network; live traffic is not yet used.

3.4BuildingsWhere the city is built up

Where the city is built up, and how densely. Footprints are summarised per hexagon as context for every site study.

Answers

Where the city is built up, and how densely.

How it is built

Overture building footprints come from the same versioned tile store as every other layer and are summarised per hexagon.

Each hexagon gets

Building count and total footprint area.

Watch for

Used as context only. It does not estimate floor space or what a building is used for.

3.5Boundaries and PIN codesThe language operations teams plan in

The language operations teams plan in. All 19,824 official PIN areas are repaired, simplified and joined to what we measure inside them.

Answers

Results in the PIN codes that operations, logistics and marketing teams already plan with.

How it is built

All 19,824 official PIN polygons are loaded and the 470 invalid shapes repaired. They are simplified into coarse 3-digit regions and fine shapes stored by tile, named after the official post office nearest the zone centre, and joined to the hexagons inside them.

Each hexagon gets

PIN code, post office, district and state, with places and residents summed per PIN.

Watch for

The PIN shapes are held internally while their licence terms are confirmed.

3.6Context and eventsWhat shifts demand for a day or a week

What shifts demand for a day or a week. Calendar and weather inputs are graded by strength of evidence before they move a forecast.

Answers

What moves demand for a day or a week: festivals, holidays and weather.

How it is built

Calendar, weather and the city snapshot are assembled for the event window. Each input is graded into an evidence tier, demand is shifted by zone and by hour against a normal week, and a brief is written with its tier beside every claim.

Each hexagon gets

Demand shift by zone, an hourly profile and the evidence tier behind it.

Watch for

Footfall in event briefs is modelled, not observed.

4MethodologySix stages from a brief to a released decisionWhat happens at each stage, who decides, and what it hands on

People make the two judgements that matter: what the business needs, and whether the result holds up. Everything between them is automated and repeatable.

5Revenue calculatorStore choice, not a weighted scoreThe store-choice formula, the calculation step by step, and an interactive calculator

The forecast comes from a spatial interaction model in the tradition of Huff (1963) and multiplicative competitive interaction. Residents of each hexagon choose among every store they can reach; a store's pull rises with its attractiveness and falls with travel time.

Pij = Aj (1 + γ Cj) dij−β ÷ [ U0 + Σk Ak (1 + γ Ck) dik−β ] P: probability that residents of cell i choose store j · A: attractiveness · C: strength of complementary businesses nearby · γ: how much complements lift a store · d: travel time · β: how fast distance deters · U₀: the outside option, demand no modelled store captures.

6Uncertainty and explanationA range a committee can questionHow ranges are simulated and how every score breaks into its drivers

Several inputs are uncertain: the decay rate, the strength of rivals and complements, the size of the outside option. The engine samples them many times and reports the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles. Alongside, the suitability score breaks into its weighted drivers, so a disagreement becomes a disagreement about a stated assumption.

7City intelligenceOrganised retail thins sharply down the tiersWhat 120 city snapshots show, and how each one is built and kept current

Each of the 120 city snapshots applies the same method inside a fixed radius, 10 km for Tier 1 and 8, 6 or 5 km for Tiers 2 to 4, and compares the city with a benchmark. Identical methods make cities comparable.

8Data APIThe same data, inside the client's own systemsWhy the API exists, the path of one call, and what each layer returns

Reports answer one question at a time. Larger clients want our evidence in their BI dashboards, GIS and their own models, across many cities at once. The Data API opens the same city files the dashboard reads, read-only, so the two can never disagree.
