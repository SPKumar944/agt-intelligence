The concept

Start from the market, not the shortlist.

Most site searches begin with whatever a broker has available. That answers "is this site good?" but never "is this where we should be?".

Whitespace analysis turns the question round. It defines who your customers are, measures where they are concentrated across the whole metro area, subtracts the demand your category already serves, and leaves the gaps: places with many of your customers and too few stores to serve them.

The result is a map of opportunity, not a list of addresses. Pockets are ranked by the unserved demand they hold, then handed to site search — so brokers are briefed on where to look, and every candidate arrives already inside a gap.

The same scan reveals where a network is over-served: clusters of stores competing for the same customers, which are candidates for consolidation or relocation.

    The opportunity model

    Demand, coverage and the gap between them.

    Every hexagon gets a demand score for the target segment and a coverage score from the existing stores that can reach it. Opportunity is the demand left over.

    Coverage decays with travel time from each store, at a rate calibrated to how far customers of the format typically travel. Pockets are formed by clustering high-opportunity cells and ranked by the unserved demand they contain.

    Opportunity per hexagon
    Oh = Dh · ( 1 − Ch )
    Dh
    target-customer demand in hexagon h (households, workers or spend)
    Ch
    coverage by existing stores, between 0 and 1
    Coverage
    Ch = maxs exp( −( ths / λ )2 )
    ths
    travel time from hexagon h to store s
    λ
    typical travel tolerance for the format, calibrated from trade areas

    Method

    From a customer definition to a ranked pocket list.

    Define the target customer

    From your best stores' trade areas — or your own customer data — we identify the segments that over-index: age bands, household types, workers, spending power.

    Measure demand everywhere

    Segment counts and spend are estimated for every hexagon in the metro area from population aggregates, daytime population and mobility.

    Map supply

    Your stores and competitors in the category are matched from places data, with coverage decaying by travel time.

    Find and rank pockets

    High-opportunity cells are clustered into pockets and ranked by unserved demand, with a minimum size so pockets can support a store.

    Hand over to site search

    Each pocket comes with its demand profile and the streets with the best frontage footfall inside it.

    FAQ

    Questions about whitespace.

    How large an area can you scan?

    An entire metro area at 65 m resolution, using coarser hexagons first and refining where opportunity is high. The scan is limited by data coverage rather than size.

    Can we define our own target customer?

    Yes. Segments can be defined from your own customer data, from your best stores' trade areas, or by hand — for example "households with children, upper-middle income".

    Does whitespace account for competitors?

    Yes. Coverage includes competitors in your category, weighted by their attractiveness, so a pocket is only flagged if customers are under-served by everyone.
