The concept

A score ranks. A forecast sizes.

Two different questions sit behind every site decision, and they need two different answers.

"Is this a better site than that one?" is answered by a site score. Each driver — footfall, co-tenancy, income, access, competition — is measured and converted to a percentile against every other location in the market. The score is a weighted average of those percentiles. It is relative, comparable across a city, and needs no sales history.

"How much will it sell?" is answered by a revenue forecast. That requires learning from real stores: a model trained on the performance of existing outlets, their trade areas and their surroundings, which then predicts sales for a candidate site in rupees.

Both are explained the same way. The score shows each driver's weight and percentile; the forecast shows how much each driver adds to or subtracts from a format baseline. And both are published with their sources, so the conversation is about assumptions rather than trust.

Established approaches

Choosing the right forecasting method.

Site forecasting has four well-established families of methods. The right one depends mostly on how many stores you have and how comparable they are.

ApproachHow it worksBest whenLimitations
AnalogueFind existing stores whose trade areas most resemble the candidate and use their sales as the estimate.Few stores, strong local knowledge.Depends on how good the analogues are; hard to explain differences.
RegressionFit sales to trade-area variables — population, income, footfall, competition — with explicit coefficients.Dozens of stores; need simple, auditable drivers.Misses interactions and non-linear effects.
Gravity / HuffModel how customers in each area split their spend between stores by distance and attractiveness.Dense markets with strong competition; cannibalisation questions.Needs calibrated distance decay and attractiveness.
Machine learningLearn non-linear relationships from many features and stores; explain each prediction with attributions.Larger networks and rich features.Needs enough stores; must be explained to be trusted.
Our default is a blend. A transparent weighted score and a gravity model from day one; a trained revenue model (PyTorch) once the network is large enough to learn from — with the score kept as its explainable baseline.

Uncertainty

Ranges, not false precision.

A single number invites false confidence. We forecast quantiles: P50 is the central estimate, P10 the level the store should beat nine times in ten, P90 the upside it should reach only one time in ten.

Ranges widen with distance from what the model has seen — a new format, an unusual neighbourhood, a ramp-up period — and narrow as comparable stores accumulate. Committees can then size the downside explicitly: does the P10 still clear the hurdle rate?

Method

From features to a forecast you can defend.

Build features per hexagon

Footfall by daypart, residents and workers by segment, spending power, co-tenants, competitors and access — computed once in the feature store and shared by every model.

Describe each store's trade area

Features are aggregated over each existing store's dynamic catchment, weighted by where its customers come from.

Train and back-test

Models are trained on store performance with point-in-time features and tested on stores held out of training, reporting error (such as MAPE) and how often outcomes fall inside the P10–P90 band.

Explain every prediction

Additive attributions split each forecast into driver contributions that sum back to the prediction, so nothing is hidden in the model.

Monitor after opening

Actual sales are compared with the forecast range as stores trade; the models are retrained as the network grows.

FAQ

Questions about forecasting.

We only have a few stores. Can you still forecast?

Yes, with honest limits. With few stores we rely on the site score, analogue stores and gravity models, and widen the forecast range accordingly. As the network grows, a trained model takes over.

Who sets the weights in the score?

We propose weights in the business profile, with a written rationale, and agree them with you. Where sales data exists, weights are checked against how well they rank existing stores.

How accurate are the forecasts?

Accuracy depends on the format, the market and how many comparable stores exist, so we report it per engagement from back-testing on held-out stores — error and band coverage — rather than quoting a universal figure.

Can we see the model itself?

You see every input, every driver contribution and the back-test results. Model artefacts can be shared under the engagement agreement.
