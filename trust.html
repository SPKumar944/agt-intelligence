Trust & data

Aggregated people. Named sources. Labelled models.

Location intelligence is only as trustworthy as the data behind it and the care taken with it. These are the principles we work to, the sources we use and the things we will not do.

Principles

Four commitments behind every engagement.

01

Aggregate only

People enter our models only as counts — by hexagon, time window, age band or household type — never as individuals.

02

Named sources

Every dataset is catalogued with its origin, date, licence and the transformations applied.

03

Labelled models

Measured and modelled numbers are always distinguished, with ranges where we estimate.

04

Client data isolated

Your stores and sales are used only for your engagement, under your agreement.

Privacy

What we never handle.

Location data is sensitive. Our models do not need to know who anyone is, so we make sure they never can.

  • No individual movement traces. Mobility data reaches us aggregated to hexagons and time windows; cells below a minimum count are suppressed.
  • No names, identifiers or contact details. Population sources are used only as counts by age band, gender and household type. Names, ID numbers, relatives and addresses are never loaded, printed or exported.
  • No re-identification. We do not join datasets in ways designed to single out people or households.
  • No sensitive inference. We do not model religion, caste, health status or similar characteristics of people.
Aggregation happens before analysis. The smallest unit we hold about people is a count in a hexagon or a time window — never a row that describes a person.

Responsible use

Estimates are for decisions, not certainty.

Our footfall, trade areas and forecasts are model estimates. They are designed to rank and compare locations consistently and to make assumptions explicit — not to replace judgement or local knowledge.

Every report labels which numbers are measured and which are modelled, states the ranges around forecasts, and lists what should be verified on the ground before a lease is signed — typically pedestrian counts at the shortlisted frontages during the hours that matter.

Interactive examples on this website are illustrative: they use synthetic data and do not represent any client.
